Abstract

We present REALM-SLAM, a real-time absolute-scale monocular SLAM system that delivers competitive metric accuracy at frame rate from a single camera, without ground-truth scale or auxiliary sensors. A classical keypoint tracker stays in the real-time loop while one frozen foundation depth backbone runs asynchronously alongside it, yielding metric trajectories at roughly 20 Hz on standard indoor benchmarks. In the back end, foundation depth factors enter local bundle adjustment jointly with multi-view reprojections under an explicit information budget, anchoring metric scale and limiting neural depth noise, together with gauge-consistent online rescaling. In the front end, the same backbone serves two roles without adding external semantic networks: its encoder tokens feed a lightweight recurrent temporal dynamic head to filter independently moving objects, while its depth outputs densify weak keyframes on demand to prevent tracking loss. Under oracle-free SE(3) evaluation across four benchmarks, REALM-SLAM attains a median ATE of 1.71 cm on TUM RGB-D, 3.46 cm on BONN Dynamic, and 46.2 cm on EuRoC, along with an average ATE of 12.67 cm on 7-Scenes, matching or outperforming competitive metric baselines while running substantially faster.

System Architecture

One frozen depth backbone serves three roles. Network: an asynchronous encoder pass per frame feeds the temporal dynamic head, and full depth inference runs per keyframe. Geometry: an ORB-SLAM3 tracker stays in the real-time loop; weak keyframes are seeded from depth on demand and the map is reborn from depth after tracking loss. Scale: budgeted depth factors in local BA and a gauge-consistent rescale keep the trajectory metric.
